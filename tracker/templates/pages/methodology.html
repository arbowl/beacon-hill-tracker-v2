Methodology

How the Tracker works

Every finding on this site comes from public pages on malegislature.gov, is checked against the verbatim text of the Legislature's own rules, and links back to the evidence it relied on. The Tracker reports what the public record shows; it does not grade or rank anyone.

Bills and bill segments

A bill is a single numbered piece of legislation, such as H10. A bill segment is that bill as one committee saw it. When H10 is referred to the Judiciary and later to House Ways and Means, it has two segments, and each committee's segment is checked separately.

Most figures on the dashboard count bill segments. When the Tracker says a number of bills were affected, each bill is counted once, no matter how many of its segments had a detected violation.

Sometimes a bill appears on a committee's list but the bill history does not show it being referred there. The Tracker lists that segment as Provisional, with the note "referral not found in the bill history", and checks no requirements for it until the referral appears.

Some referrals owe nothing under the rules tracked here. The committees on Bills in the Third Reading, for example, only examine and correct bills ordered to a third reading (House Rule 22, Senate Rule 33), and an open House referral owes a posted vote only once the committee reports. These referrals are shown on the bill's page as No applicable requirements, with the reason. They are not bill segments: they have no label and are left out of every count and rate.

When a committee folds several bills into one study order or new draft, the bills it accompanies can travel with it to the next committee, often the House or Senate Rules committee, without being referred there themselves. Those bills are shown on their page as No applicable requirements too, with a link to the study order or draft, which is the document checked under that committee's rules. A new draft substituted on the floor for a bill ordered to a third reading is treated the same way: the committee on Bills in the Third Reading held only the bill it replaced, which is linked instead.

Rarely, the legislative record itself leaves out how a bill came before a committee, for example a transfer between committees that was never entered in the bill history. Each such case is reviewed by hand. The bill is shown as No applicable requirements with a note explaining what the record omits, and nothing about that referral is judged.

The four requirements

RequirementWhat the Tracker checksRules
Hearing noticeThe hearing was announced at least 10 days in advance.JR 1D, SR 12, HR 17A
SummaryA plain-language summary of the bill was posted.JR 1B
VoteThe committee's vote on the bill was posted.JR 1D, HR 17B
Reported outThe committee reported the bill by its deadline.JR 10, HR 27

A requirement applies only once the event that triggers it has happened. A bill that has not had a hearing has no hearing-notice requirement yet, so it shows Not applicable for it.

Labels and rates

Each bill segment gets one of three labels. No violation detected: every requirement that applies is detected as met. Violation detected: the Tracker detected at least one violation. Provisional: anything else, such as a deadline that has not arrived.

Each requirement on a segment is shown as Met, Pending (not yet determinable, for example because the deadline has not arrived), Not shown (the public record does not show the requirement being met, which the Tracker counts as a detected violation), or Not applicable.

A detected violation means the public record does not show the requirement being met. It may reflect a gap in what was posted online rather than in what the committee did.

The headline compliance rate is the share of segments with no detected violation: those labeled No violation detected plus those labeled Provisional, divided by all segments. Provisional segments count toward the rate because nothing in the public record shows a violation for them yet; they keep their own label so readers can set them aside. Data files and URLs use the short names Compliant, Provisional, and Non-Compliant for the three labels.

Why "provisional"

A requirement is pending when its deadline has not arrived, when the timing is ambiguous, when the data needed is missing, or when a document needs review. Pending items are not counted as violations. Each one says why it is pending in its audit trail.

Reading the charts

The attrition funnel follows bill segments through the requirements in the order a bill meets them. Each stage counts the segments with every requirement so far either met or not yet applicable. Because a requirement that has not applied counts as passed, a committee that has heard few bills shows little drop at the hearing-notice stage. The last stage equals the number of segments with no violation detected.

The range across committees uses a stricter measure: the share of determined segments with every applicable requirement met, leaving provisional segments out. It is drawn as one unnamed dot per committee, to show how widely the figures vary without singling any committee out. Names appear on hover and in the committee table. Committees with fewer than 20 determined segments are not drawn.

The change figures on the compliance rate compare it with the most recent update at least 24 hours and at least 7 days earlier.

Limits

  • The Tracker sees only what malegislature.gov publishes. A document posted somewhere else, or posted and later removed, may not be found.
  • When a search for a document comes up empty, the audit trail says whether every source was checked or the search was incomplete. An incomplete search is not proof of absence.
  • Rule interpretations are the Tracker's. Each derivation step cites the rule text it relied on, so the reasoning can be checked and disputed.
  • Nothing on this site is a legal finding, and none of it states or implies why a requirement was or was not met.

Sources and updates

The Tracker reads bill histories, hearing pages, and committee documents on malegislature.gov. Every page it reads is stored, so any finding can be re-checked against exactly what the site said at the time. The data is updated once a day, usually overnight; the date of each committee's data is on the committee table.

Every change to how the Tracker collects and checks the record is listed on the changes page, with the date it took effect.

Data downloads

Signed-in readers can download the full current dataset from their watchlist. Both files are rebuilt after every update, and each is listed with its sha256 so a copy can be checked.

current.csv.gz has one row per bill segment. Its columns use the Tracker's internal names, which map to the labels on this site as follows:

current.json.gz has every committee's complete record in the format the Tracker publishes: each bill segment's results with their derivation steps, evidence, and the verbatim rule text they cite.
