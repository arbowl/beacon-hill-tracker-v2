Methodology

How the Tracker works

Every finding on this site comes from public pages on malegislature.gov, is checked against the verbatim text of the Legislature's own rules, and links back to the evidence it relied on. The Tracker reports what the public record shows; it does not grade or rank anyone.

Bills and bill segments

A bill is a single numbered piece of legislation, such as H10. A bill segment is that bill as one committee saw it. When H10 is referred to the Judiciary and later to House Ways and Means, it has two segments, and each committee is judged only on its own segment.

Most figures on the dashboard count bill segments. When the Tracker says a number of bills were affected, each bill is counted once, no matter how many of its segments had a detected violation.

Labels and rates

Each bill segment gets one of three labels. No violation detected: every requirement that applies is detected as met. Violation detected: the Tracker detected at least one violation. Provisional: anything else, such as a deadline that has not arrived. A requirement that does not apply to a segment, such as hearing notice for a bill that was never heard, is shown as Not applicable and does not affect the label. A detected violation means the public record does not show the requirement being met; it may reflect a gap in what was posted online rather than in what the committee did. The headline compliance rate is the share of segments with no detected violation: those labeled No violation detected plus those labeled Provisional, divided by all segments. The site and its data files keep the short names Compliant, Provisional, and Non-Compliant for these labels.

Each requirement on a segment is shown as Met, Pending (not yet determinable, for example because the deadline has not arrived), Not shown (the public record does not show the requirement being met, which the Tracker counts as a detected violation), or Not applicable.

The range across committees uses a stricter measure: the share of determined segments with every applicable requirement met, leaving provisional segments out. It is drawn as one unnamed dot per committee, to show how widely the figures vary without singling any committee out. Names appear on hover and in the full committee table.

Why "provisional"

A requirement is provisional when its deadline has not arrived, when the timing is ambiguous, when the data needed is missing, or when a document needs review. Provisional items are not counted as violations.

Sources

The Tracker reads bill histories, hearing pages, and committee documents on malegislature.gov. Every page it reads is stored, so any finding can be re-checked against exactly what the site said at the time.
